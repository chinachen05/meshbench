Field note 05 / Measurements

Estimate how much spline cord to buy

Measure the groove path, then add a clearly stated waste reserve.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

Spline is the flexible cord that traps screening inside the frame channel. Ordering enough spline is a length problem; finding a fitting diameter is a separate product-compatibility problem. The perimeter gives a useful first estimate for ordinary rectangular panels.

What to inspect first

  • Measure along the channel centerline where the spline actually sits, not around the outside of a decorative frame lip.
  • Count each separate screened panel. A door with a separate upper and lower mesh section has more groove length than one simple rectangle.
  • For nonrectangular or curved openings, trace the groove with a flexible tape rather than relying on width and height alone.

A practical sequence

  1. For a rectangular panel, calculate twice the width plus twice the height in matching units.
  2. Multiply by the number of matching panels, then add a purchasing reserve for corner trimming and mistakes.
  3. Use the Spline Length Bench to compare the computed requirement against spool lengths.
  4. Choose diameter separately using the frame groove, old sample and mesh material specification.

Worked situation

A rectangular groove path 70 cm wide and 110 cm high has a perimeter of 360 cm. Four such panels require 14.4 m before reserve; a 10% allowance brings the estimate to 15.84 m.

Common wrong turn

Purchasing 16 meters of the wrong diameter is not a successful plan. Length calculations never confirm compatibility with the channel.

When to stop:

If the frame uses a locking strip instead of round spline, or if the spline groove is damaged, seek product-specific hardware guidance.
