Field note 06 / Measurements

Choose spline diameter without guessing

A thicker screen often needs a different spline than a standard fiberglass screen.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

Spline diameter depends on channel size and screening thickness, not on the outer dimensions of the panel. Phifer’s manufacturer chart advises measuring the spline groove and consulting the specific fabric guidance; certain thick specialty fabrics require a smaller spline than standard mesh in comparable frames.

What to inspect first

  • Measure the empty groove width with a suitable gauge or calipers; a soft old cord may no longer show its original diameter.
  • Identify the exact screening material. Aluminum, thick pet-resistant fabric and solar screening may follow different recommendations.
  • Inspect for accumulated paint, debris and groove damage before deciding that a cord is too large.

A practical sequence

  1. Keep a short undamaged sample of the removed spline with the panel label.
  2. Compare groove measurement and fabric type with the manufacturer’s current spline chart.
  3. Test a small section without forcing the roller; the cord should seat securely without crushing the frame.
  4. If fabric is pinched or the rail distorts, remove the test and recheck the selection rather than pushing harder.

Worked situation

A frame previously holding thin fiberglass may not accept that same cord with a much thicker polyester pet mesh. The correct answer comes from the mesh supplier’s compatible groove-and-spline combination.

Common wrong turn

Using the old cord as an infallible reference ignores compression and material changes; likewise, a universal “one size fits all” recommendation is unreliable.

When to stop:

Stop if a groove is split, a frame bows under light pressure, or the proposed spline is not listed for that fabric. Seek fabrication guidance.
