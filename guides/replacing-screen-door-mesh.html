Field note 25 / Hardware

Rescreen a patio door without distorting it

Door panels are larger and harder to keep square than typical window frames.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

A tall screen door offers more working area, but the long rails can move under fabric tension. The installation principle resembles window rescreening: remove old spline, support the frame, align new material, roll in compatible cord and trim surplus after seating.

What to inspect first

  • Measure the screened opening and cut margin before removing the old material.
  • Verify door rollers, latch and frame corners are sound; there is little value in new mesh on a failing door.
  • Choose a sufficiently long work surface so neither side hangs unsupported.

A practical sequence

  1. Remove the door following its manufacturer’s procedure and protect nearby glass.
  2. Secure the frame squarely on a flat surface, keeping the channel accessible.
  3. Lay fabric with spare edges and roll compatible spline progressively, checking rail straightness.
  4. Refit the door, then test sliding, latch alignment and perimeter gaps rather than judging only the appearance of the mesh.

Worked situation

A door with a 760 × 1850 mm screened opening and 30 mm chosen margin per edge needs an initial cut about 820 × 1910 mm, before checking available roll widths.

Common wrong turn

Working with the door leaning against a wall encourages the frame to twist and leaves the fabric uneven when it is reinstalled.

When to stop:

Large doors that need lifting over a head-height track or removing glass-side hardware may be better handled by an installer.
