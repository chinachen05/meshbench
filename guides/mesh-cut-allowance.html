Field note 04 / Measurements

How much extra mesh to leave for cutting

Start with a deliberate overlap and trim only after securing the spline.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

Screen fabric needs spare material beyond the retention groove so you can align it and keep control while rolling in spline. The necessary working allowance depends on material, opening and installer preference. It is not a dimension to subtract from the completed panel.

What to inspect first

  • Measure the full screened opening rather than only its visible center.
  • Check whether you can lay the removed frame flat and keep fabric evenly aligned before committing to a cut.
  • Look at the fabric roll width: a cut with overlap must fit across the roll or be rotated for a better orientation.

A practical sequence

  1. Choose a practical per-side allowance and enter it in the Mesh Cut Planner; the default is explicitly an editable planning assumption.
  2. Cut with straight edges and cover the groove on all four sides.
  3. Keep the loose overlap visible as spline is rolled around the corners.
  4. Trim the surplus only after the screen lies flat and the spline is seated.

Worked situation

For a 500 × 800 mm groove-bounded opening and 20 mm of overlap per edge, the initial fabric rectangle is 540 × 840 mm. A 900 mm roll accommodates either 540 or 840 mm across.

Common wrong turn

Precutting fabric flush to the channel before rolling leaves nothing to hold and can lead to a pulled-out corner.

When to stop:

Use the screen maker’s cut guidelines for unusually thick, specialty or large patio fabrics; the standard window workflow may not transfer directly.
