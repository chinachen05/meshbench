Field note 32 / Care & safety

What to check when a room feels less breezy

Mesh, dirt, the window opening and weather all affect perceived airflow.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

A room may feel less ventilated after a mesh change, but it is difficult to assign the cause to fabric density alone. Window opening area, wind direction, interior doors, dust in the mesh and even outdoor temperature can change airflow. Product openness matters, yet it is not a stand-alone airflow measurement.

What to inspect first

  • Compare the same window position before and after cleaning, when conditions are reasonably similar.
  • Check that the sash opens fully and no screen frame rail blocks part of the intended passage.
  • Review the mesh product’s published openness and intended use rather than inferring performance from appearance.

A practical sequence

  1. Clean a dusty panel using the manufacturer’s approved method.
  2. Observe ventilation with interior doors open and closed; record conditions without claiming a laboratory measurement.
  3. If you switch to fine or solar mesh, test one panel first before replacing the rest.
  4. Consider a ventilation specialist if the room has persistent moisture or combustion-related concerns.

Worked situation

A bathroom window feels stagnant after installing fine mesh, but the sash also opens only halfway due to a stuck hinge. Repairing the hinge and cleaning the panel should be checked before blaming the weave.

Common wrong turn

Calculating air changes per hour from mesh count alone is unreliable without actual airflow measurements.

When to stop:

Where air quality or ventilation is a health or building-code issue, consult a qualified specialist; insect mesh is not an air-cleaning device.
