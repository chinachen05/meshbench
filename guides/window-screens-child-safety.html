Field note 29 / Care & safety

Window screens are not child-fall guards

Screening keeps insects out; it is not designed to restrain a child.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

An insect screen can appear firmly attached yet release under outward pressure. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission explicitly warns never to rely on window screens to prevent falls. Preventing access and using appropriate window guards or stops are separate safety measures.

What to inspect first

  • Look for furniture children might climb near a window, including beds, sofas and storage units.
  • Identify which windows can open wide and what approved guards or stops are installed.
  • Understand any emergency escape requirements before adding a guard.

A practical sequence

  1. Keep climbable furniture away from accessible windows.
  2. Use properly installed window guards or stops appropriate for the window, child and local safety requirements.
  3. Check the condition of any existing guard and make sure adult emergency opening requirements are maintained where applicable.
  4. Treat screen maintenance as insect control only; replacing or tightening mesh never substitutes for fall prevention.

Worked situation

A screen snaps securely into a second-floor bedroom window. That says nothing about whether it can withstand a child leaning against it; a properly chosen guard or stop must address the fall risk.

Common wrong turn

Telling a family that pet-resistant or heavy-duty insect mesh is a “child safety screen” is unsafe unless a separately tested protective system is specified.

When to stop:

If an accessible high window has no suitable fall-prevention hardware, speak with building management or a qualified installer before relying on that opening.
