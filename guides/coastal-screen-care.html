Field note 13 / Materials

Choose and care for screens in salty or humid locations

Salt exposure affects frames and hardware as much as fabric.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

In coastal and humid settings, corrosion of springs, screws and frame rails can be the first reason a screen fails. Fiberglass mesh may remain intact while a steel clip rusts or an aluminum corner weakens. Material selection should include the entire assembled screen, not just its central fabric.

What to inspect first

  • Look for white or rusty deposits on joints, clips and rail ends.
  • Check whether the screen can still be removed easily without forcing corroded hardware.
  • Find the window maker’s recommendations for coastal maintenance and compatible replacement parts.

A practical sequence

  1. Rinse salt deposits using gentle methods approved for the frame and let the assembly dry.
  2. Replace visibly compromised clips or fasteners with manufacturer-compatible corrosion-resistant parts.
  3. Avoid abrasive brushes that scratch coatings and create corrosion points.
  4. Recheck retention after cleaning, especially on panels exposed to wind.

Worked situation

A balcony screen has intact fiberglass mesh but a rusty spring that no longer pushes the frame into its track. Replacing only the mesh would leave the retention failure unresolved.

Common wrong turn

Treating all screen rails with an arbitrary oil or chemical spray can harm finishes; follow the maker’s product-specific care guidance.

When to stop:

If corrosion compromises a balcony, high window or external assembly, arrange professional inspection and do not lean outside to reach it.
