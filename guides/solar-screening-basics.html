Field note 11 / Materials

Solar-control mesh is not ordinary insect mesh

Understand shading and ventilation effects before swapping materials.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

Solar-control screening is intended to reduce some solar gain and glare, often by using a thicker or denser construction than basic insect screening. That can alter airflow, outward visibility and the look of a room. Different solar products offer different properties.

What to inspect first

  • Identify the actual problem: glare, afternoon heat, privacy or insects. These are not interchangeable goals.
  • Check whether your supplier provides openness, shading and installation details for the precise product.
  • Measure the window orientation and notice when sunlight arrives before choosing a permanent darker panel.

A practical sequence

  1. Test a material swatch against the glass at the time you want relief from sun.
  2. Verify that the frame and groove can accept the fabric thickness and recommended spline.
  3. Compare the new view and ventilation when the window is open.
  4. Do not block egress or window hardware with an improvised exterior panel.

Worked situation

A west-facing office gets bright low-angle sun after 3 p.m. A solar mesh sample may be worth testing, but a north-facing bedroom without glare probably has different priorities.

Common wrong turn

Assuming every “80” or “90” label means the same shade percentage across brands can lead to mismatched performance expectations.

When to stop:

Consult glazing or building professionals for quantified thermal savings; mesh selection alone cannot reliably estimate an energy-bill reduction.
