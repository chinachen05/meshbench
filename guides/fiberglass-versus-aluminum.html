Field note 07 / Materials

Fiberglass vs aluminum insect mesh

Compare handling, visibility, denting and the environment before buying.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

Fiberglass and aluminum screening can both keep common flying insects out when installed correctly, but they behave differently during fabrication and everyday use. Fiberglass is generally forgiving when you roll it into a channel. Aluminum wire can kink or crease, and that damage may remain visible.

What to inspect first

  • Describe the panel’s exposure: window, high-traffic patio door, cooking area or coastal environment.
  • Check whether a pet or frequently handled door puts the panel under repeated impact.
  • Compare actual product labels for openness, corrosion resistance and intended framing; do not assume all “metal screen” products are alike.

A practical sequence

  1. For a first-time repair, practice on a short spare section before working on the full frame.
  2. When using aluminum, support the mesh to reduce kinking and follow the maker’s advice about roller side and corner treatment.
  3. For standard fiberglass, aim for even alignment rather than maximum tension.
  4. Examine visibility from inside at the window’s usual daylight angle before ordering a full-house roll.

Worked situation

A small bedroom window with an existing fiberglass screen may be easiest to rescreen in the same material. A workshop panel exposed to sharp objects needs a separate discussion about durability and possible protective alternatives.

Common wrong turn

Choosing aluminum solely because it sounds stronger can produce a visibly creased panel if the installation technique does not match the material.

When to stop:

If the application involves fire, industrial impacts, security or animal containment, ordinary insect screening is not a certified protective barrier.
