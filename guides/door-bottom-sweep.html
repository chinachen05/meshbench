Field note 24 / Hardware

Inspect the bottom sweep of a screen door

The space beneath the door is another insect entry path.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

A bottom brush or sweep can close ordinary threshold gaps, but it must match the door style and leave the sliding or hinged mechanism operating freely. A missing sweep may be mistaken for a mesh failure because insects enter near floor level.

What to inspect first

  • With the door closed, look for light beneath the bottom rail.
  • Check whether the bottom has a brush, flexible fin, metal strip or no seal by design.
  • Watch for worn spots, debris and threshold changes that create a gap only at one part of travel.

A practical sequence

  1. Photograph the bottom rail profile and measure the existing sweep before shopping.
  2. Clean the sill and verify the door is correctly adjusted first.
  3. Select a compatible replacement that does not snag or jam as the door opens.
  4. Close the door repeatedly and confirm that the sweep contacts the intended threshold without curling.

Worked situation

A screen door leaves a 4 mm gap along most of its lower edge while the mesh shows no tears. A worn brush may be the cause, but first make sure the door is not riding too high on its rollers.

Common wrong turn

An overly thick seal can make a door difficult to close and can damage a light frame when forced.

When to stop:

For accessible exits or commercial doors, consult the door maker or building specialist before modifying thresholds.
