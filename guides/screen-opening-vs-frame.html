Field note 02 / Measurements

Frame size vs mesh opening: avoid the mix-up

Know which rectangle you are measuring before cutting material.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

Three similar-looking dimensions serve different purposes: the finished outer frame, the visible mesh area and the spline-channel perimeter. A replacement frame is sized to the window; a cut piece of screening is sized to overlap the mesh channel; spline quantity is based on the path around that channel. Mixing these up is a common reason for wasted mesh.

What to inspect first

  • Look for the narrow recessed groove that accepts spline. Its inside boundary defines the area you need to cover with fabric.
  • Measure the width and height of the area to be screened, then decide a working margin outside the groove for gripping and trimming.
  • If the frame has curved corners or a divided center bar, sketch the exact path before calculating material.

A practical sequence

  1. Write three labels on the work sheet: outside frame, screened opening, and groove path.
  2. Use the Mesh Cut Planner with the screened opening and your chosen per-side working margin, not the finished outer frame unless they happen to match.
  3. Use the Spline Length calculator for the full groove perimeter. Do not infer spline diameter from perimeter length.
  4. Lay the fabric on the actual frame before cutting; check it covers every corner with room to hold the edges.

Worked situation

A 560 × 760 mm screened opening with 25 mm of working overlap per side needs an initial cut at 610 × 810 mm. That says nothing about the outer frame dimensions.

Common wrong turn

Adding the same margin to a replacement frame order and to a piece of mesh will oversize the frame but is normal for the mesh cut.

When to stop:

If the channel is hidden under a retaining strip or the frame has a nonstandard seal, use the product-specific installation instructions rather than forcing standard spline into it.
