Field note 23 / Hardware

Close the gaps around a screen door latch

Look for frame alignment and weather gaps, not only mesh holes.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

Insects often enter at an imperfectly closing screen door even when its central mesh is intact. A misaligned strike, worn latch, uneven rollers or distorted frame can leave a visible gap near the handle. The right fix depends on the source of the gap.

What to inspect first

  • Close the door without force and look along its entire leading edge against daylight.
  • Check whether the latch tongue engages fully in the strike plate.
  • Compare top and bottom clearances; an uneven pattern may indicate roller height rather than latch hardware.

A practical sequence

  1. Clean the track and confirm the door moves without binding.
  2. Check alignment with the maker’s roller and latch adjustment method.
  3. Inspect flexible seals or brushes for missing lengths and replace them only with compatible parts.
  4. Perform a final light check around the entire perimeter after the door closes normally.

Worked situation

A vertical 6 mm light gap near the latch narrows to 1 mm at the bottom. This is a useful clue that the door is tilted, not that you need denser mesh.

Common wrong turn

Sticking self-adhesive foam wherever light appears can prevent the door from latching or interfere with emergency use.

When to stop:

Seek service when the door frame is twisted, the latch cannot secure the door or the track is physically damaged.
