Field note 30 / Care & safety

A whole-home mosquito screen inspection

Combine screen maintenance with broader mosquito control steps.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

CDC recommends installing, repairing and using door and window screens, but screening is only one part of keeping mosquitoes out. Open doors, unscreened vents and standing water around the home all affect the result. A house can have perfect windows and still permit mosquitoes to enter.

What to inspect first

  • Survey every window and external door, not just the rooms where bites are noticed.
  • Look for holes, missing spline, loose frame corners and unsealed door edges.
  • Inspect water-holding containers nearby according to local public-health advice.

A practical sequence

  1. Make a simple room-by-room list of problem openings and photograph the defects.
  2. Patch isolated holes or rescreen badly damaged panels, then recheck the perimeter.
  3. Keep garage and exterior doors closed when not actively passing through.
  4. Empty and scrub standing-water containers regularly, following CDC recommendations.

Worked situation

A family replaces all bedroom mesh but regularly leaves a utility door open at dusk. Closing that entry and addressing standing water may matter more than installing a second layer of mesh.

Common wrong turn

Choosing very fine mesh while leaving a finger-width door gap is not a comprehensive exclusion plan.

When to stop:

Contact local mosquito-control or pest professionals when infestations persist, and use public-health advice for bite prevention and illness concerns.
