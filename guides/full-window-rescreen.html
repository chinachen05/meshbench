Field note 15 / Repairs

Rescreen a removable window frame step by step

Work on a flat supported frame with an even mesh overlap.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

Full rescreening is usually simpler when the existing frame is intact and can be safely removed from inside the home. The work involves separating old spline and mesh, laying new material squarely, rolling retention cord into its channel and trimming excess only after the screen is secured.

What to inspect first

  • Confirm that the frame is removable without leaning outdoors and that all retention clips are accounted for.
  • Check that its corners are square and the groove is clean before cutting expensive mesh.
  • Verify fabric and spline compatibility using the product maker’s chart.

A practical sequence

  1. Lay the frame on a flat work surface and secure it so the rails cannot bow.
  2. Lift out one end of old spline carefully, remove it and discard damaged mesh; avoid gouging the channel.
  3. Position the new piece with working margin on every edge and align the weave before rolling.
  4. Roll spline in steadily, seating corners without excessive force; cut excess fabric away from the groove afterward.

Worked situation

If an opening is 620 × 820 mm and you choose 25 mm allowance per edge, cut approximately 670 × 870 mm to begin. Verify the actual overlap on the frame before trimming.

Common wrong turn

Pulling hard on the free fabric while inserting spline can curve slender frame rails inward and create diagonal wrinkles.

When to stop:

Use professional help if the panel is high above ground, part of a large pool enclosure, or has damaged structural frame pieces.
