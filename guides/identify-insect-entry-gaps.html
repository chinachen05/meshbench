Field note 26 / Hardware

Find where insects enter when the mesh looks intact

A perfect weave cannot compensate for a gap at the frame or threshold.

Editorially prepared 15 November 2026

The decision you need to make

If mosquitoes or flies still enter through a screened opening, the problem may be the surrounding assembly, not its mesh count. Gaps appear near frame corners, a door sweep, window sash joints or hardware cutouts. The entry may also be another unscreened opening elsewhere in the home.

What to inspect first

  • Check the screen at dusk with room lights on for visible gaps at the edges, while using safe indoor access.
  • Inspect torn corners and loose spline by touch only where there is no fall risk.
  • Walk through all exterior doors, vents and frequently opened passages rather than focusing on one window.

A practical sequence

  1. Confirm the door or sash closes to its intended stop.
  2. Repair visible holes and replace missing compatible seals or retention hardware.
  3. Check the final perimeter with the window or door in its usual operating position.
  4. Also remove standing water and reduce open-door time as recommended by mosquito-control guidance.

Worked situation

Insects appear indoors after sunset but the mesh field is unbroken. A 3 mm latch-side gap and a propped-open utility door are more plausible first checks than replacing the mesh with a finer weave.

Common wrong turn

Using pesticide as the only response while leaving open gaps and water containers unaddressed is unlikely to give durable control.

When to stop:

When insects persist despite the visible fixes, ask a pest-control professional to identify the species and possible routes.
